The trial that changed the argument

In this fictional journal-club scene, an editor sees five selected historical trials of intravenous magnesium after acute myocardial infarction. The much larger ISIS-4 trial arrives. Predict what happens to the pooled mortality odds ratio; then check the arithmetic yourself.

Scope: These are six deliberately selected records from the 16-trial metadat::dat.egger2001 archive, not a systematic search, published six-trial meta-analysis, or contemporary treatment recommendation. Do not interpret an odds ratio as a risk ratio. No spiritual analogy or simulated patient outcome is presented as a clinical fact.

1. Make a prediction

When ISIS-4 is added, which change do you expect for this selected equal-effects Peto calculation?

Choose an answer to reveal the methodological lesson.

Peto's method assumes an equal effect and is not universally reliable, especially with seriously unbalanced randomisation or large treatment effects. Changing study inclusion here teaches influence; it cannot identify the correct clinical model or establish eligibility.

3. Decide what the result establishes

Can the selected numerical reversal alone establish that publication bias caused the earlier apparent benefit?

4. Verify the real historical comparison

The official metadat reference example separately reports results from all fifteen trials excluding ISIS-4: Peto OR 0.55 (95% CI 0.44–0.68), and all sixteen: OR 1.01 (0.95–1.07). Those published documentation figures are not the six-record classroom numbers above.

Historical publications: Egger & Davey Smith, BMJ (1995), doi:10.1136/bmj.310.6982.752; ISIS-4 Collaborative Group, Lancet (1995), doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(95)90865-X.
