An evidence investigation in three dated acts

What happens when a treatment improves the outcome you can see on the monitor, but the outcome patients care about tells a more complicated story? Act I · Before December 1989 · The convincing surrogate

The rhythm strip looks better. Does that settle it?

The original 1989 investigators describe prophylactic lidocaine as reducing ventricular fibrillation during acute myocardial infarction. But preventing an arrhythmia does not automatically establish a reduction in death. Their randomised trials were too small individually to reliably resolve a mortality difference.

Act II · December 1989 · What the mortality evidence said

The endpoint changes the verdict

The investigators examined mortality as well as ventricular fibrillation. Their numerical mortality findings are sealed until you commit a sign prediction below. You may also choose the open-book route to view every source and result without predicting.

In the archived six-study example (subsequently tabulated by Normand in 1999), every row describes deaths during assigned treatment, not ventricular fibrillation. Before you inspect the six rows, predict the sign of their pooled mortality risk difference.

Decision 2 · For the six R-archive records, what sign do you predict?

1989 mortality reveal (a distinct 14-trial review): Hine and colleagues (1989) synthesised 14 randomised trials and reported an increased treatment-period mortality risk in their hospital-phase analysis: risk difference about +0.029, 95% CI +0.004 to +0.055. The risk-difference direction is lidocaine minus control. This subgroup report is not the same as a modern all-population treatment conclusion.

Reproduce the full six-study benchmark in native R 
library(metadat)
library(metafor)
dat <- metadat::dat.hine1989
stopifnot(nrow(dat) == 6)
d <- metafor::escalc(measure = "RD", ai = ai, n1i = n1i, ci = ci, n2i = n2i, data = dat)
fit <- metafor::rma(yi, vi, data = d, method = "REML")
print(fit)  # RD ~ +0.02944, CI [+0.00383, +0.05506]; estimated tau2 = 0

Act III · 2015 · A broader evidence base

The next surprise: a significant older subgroup is not the final population-wide answer

A 2015 Cochrane review of a broader selection reported all-cause mortality in 18 studies, 11,727 participants: RR 1.02 (95% CI 0.82–1.27). That interval includes benefit and harm. These are review-reported results, not calculations from our six rows.
