The sixth study arrived. What changed?

A group has pooled historical studies of a tuberculosis vaccine and announced a striking result. Then someone asks why a much larger trial was left out. You are the evidence editor. You must decide what to show — and what you cannot responsibly conclude.

Scene 2 / reconstruct it yourself

Six historical rows. No invented patients.

Counts are transcribed from the official metafor BCG example (dat.bcg / dat.colditz1994). Only IDs 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 and 8 of the original 13 studies are used here. This deliberately nonrepresentative selection demonstrates influence, not vaccine effectiveness.

Selected: 6 of six. Untick any row to experiment. These are post-hoc teaching scenarios and must never be misrepresented as prespecified eligibility rules.

Methods, formula and exact R reproduction

Each trial's log risk ratio is log[(tp/(tp+tn))/(cp/(cp+cn))]; sampling variance is 1/tp - 1/(tp+tn) + 1/cp - 1/(cp+cn). The fixed-effect estimate is inverse-variance weighted. The random-effects demonstration uses DerSimonian–Laird τ² and a normal-approximation interval. Small-k uncertainty and model assumptions are not fully addressed here.

library(metafor)
d <- subset(dat.bcg, trial %in% c(1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8))
e <- escalc(measure="RR", ai=tpos, bi=tneg,
            ci=cpos, di=cneg, data=d)
predict(rma(yi, vi, data=e, method="FE"), transf=exp)
predict(rma(yi, vi, data=e, method="DL"), transf=exp)
# Sensitivity exercises:
subset(e, trial != 8)
subset(e, alloc == "random")

Original source: Colditz GA et al., JAMA 1994;271:698–702. Source analysis and complete thirteen-study table. Endpoint: tuberculosis cases (vaccinated versus control). Assignment method and latitude vary. A real synthesis requires a protocol, complete eligibility and searching, risk-of-bias assessments and clinical interpretation.

Scene 3 / non-religious evidence story

What should the journal editor print?

Not “the vaccine works” or “the vaccine fails” based on these six chosen rows. Publish the trial inclusion log, explain why a large contrasting result matters, show model dependence and clinical/design heterogeneity, and retrieve the other seven rows before making substantive claims. An apparently precise pooled number is not an exemption from these duties.

Take-home detective prompt: Write two sentences: (1) What is the difference between data provenance and scientific validity? (2) What would you request before trusting a pooled clinical conclusion?

Optional / reflective storytelling · not a source of effect estimates

Three Qur’anic narratives about intellectual responsibility

These passages offer optional moral reflection for learners who value Qur’anic storytelling. The historical vaccine results above stand or fall on empirical evidence and sound methods, not on scripture. Narrative descriptions below are paraphrases, not translations quoted from the Qur’an.

The hoopoe's report

An-Naml 27:20–28, especially 27:27. Sulayman receives unexpected news about Saba' and responds by checking the report. Reflection: a dramatic abstract should invite source verification, not automatic acceptance or rejection.

Ya‘qub's continued search

Yusuf 12:87. Ya‘qub instructs his sons to seek news of Yusuf and his brother without surrendering hope. Reflection: do not confuse one unsuccessful database search with proof that evidence does not exist.

Editorial prompt: What source outside journal indexing could contain a completed trial?

The cost of careless reports

Al-Hujurat 49:6. The verse calls for verification when receiving a troubling report to avoid harm. Reflection: disseminating a flawed certainty claim can have consequences; the methodological standard still comes from research practice.
