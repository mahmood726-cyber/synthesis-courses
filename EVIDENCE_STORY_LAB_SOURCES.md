Full dataset and source: https://wviechtb.github.io/metafor/reference/escalc.html

Our page calculates inverse-variance fixed-effect and DerSimonian-Laird random-effects models on log risk ratios. The published data are historic; the subset is not suitable for a clinical recommendation.

## Interactive methods and reflective framing

The story displays a live **log-risk-ratio forest plot**, with study-level normal-approximation 95% intervals, common-effect inverse-variance pooling and a DerSimonian–Laird random-effects demonstration. All 57 possible subsets containing at least two of the six classroom records were checked to fit the plot’s RR axis (0.05–2.5). Subsetting is an influence **exercise**, not a prespecified meta-analysis or evidence of exclusion eligibility. In particular, the selected six have substantial heterogeneity and include alternate allocation: neither the plot nor the normal-model intervals establishes a current BCG effectiveness claim.

Four in-lesson evidence-editor decisions are offered after the practical exercises in modules **3, 7, 9 and 22**. The new module 9 decision addresses the insufficiency of six non-exhaustively selected trials for a reliable funnel-asymmetry diagnosis. An optional reflection cites **Al-Isra 17:36** (https://quran.com/17/36) as a paraphrased ethical analogy about epistemic caution, **not** a Qur’anic assertion about statistical testing. Belief is never tested or graded. The new story content is an English pilot visible in the 12 course routes; translations of these story passages have not been made or independently reviewed.
